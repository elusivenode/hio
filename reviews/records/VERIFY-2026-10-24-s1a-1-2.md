## F6 outcome: remediation rejected

**A failed rollback still exposes an object through the live store.** With the same setup,
inject the `os.chmod` failure for `catalogue/b` and also inject `OSError` when rollback calls
`os.unlink` on `catalogue/b`. `commit()` raises the injected chmod error. Immediately after
the raised commit, before reopening, `snapshot()` contains `catalogue/b` with SHA-256
`3e23e8160039594a33894f6564e1b1348bbd7a0088d42c4acb73eeaed59c009d`, and
`get('catalogue/b')` returns `b'b'`. A journal remains. After restoring unlink and reopening,
recovery deletes `b` and the snapshot becomes empty. This is a single-writer failure
injection. The author's new test checks that the journal survives and reopening cleans up;
it does not check visibility to a caller holding the existing store. The documented
all-or-nothing `commit()` guarantee is therefore still false after a raised commit. If the
intended contract is recoverable publication, reads and listings must fail closed while an
incomplete journal exists, or the publication boundary must hide uncommitted objects.

**Ownership is lost on recovery after a collision plus failed rollback.** I also extended
the author's competing-writer test. After this transaction writes `catalogue/a`, another
writer creates `catalogue/b=b'other writer'` before this transaction's `put(b)`, which raises
`WriteOnceViolation`. Inject an `OSError` when rollback tries to unlink this transaction's
`a`, leaving its journal. Before reopen, both `a` and the other writer's `b` exist. On reopen,
`_recover()` removes **both**, because the journal contains intended keys rather than keys
proved to belong to the transaction. This violates the author's ownership claim and destroys
a write-once object. This second probe uses concurrent writing, which the response calls a
residual assumption; it is an additional limit, not needed for the single-writer verdict.

The smallest correction for the supported single-writer path is to prevent `get`, `list` and
other reads from exposing keys covered by an unresolved journal, or to make publication atomic.
If the competing-writer guard is retained as a claim, recovery must also distinguish owned
objects from a key created by another writer; alternatively state and enforce the single-writer
contract consistently and remove that claim. Re-test the original chmod case, failed rollback
before and after reopen, and a collision with failed rollback.
