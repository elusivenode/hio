## F6 outcome: remediation rejected

**A short journal write permits an unclaimed object to be linked and later exposed.** The new
`_journal_claim` calls `os.write(fd, line)` once, does not check its returned byte count, then
fsyncs and allows `os.link`. `_claims` silently ignores a malformed final claim line. I tested
these two behaviours together in a child process:

1. Pre-create `catalogue/`; stage `catalogue/a=b'a'` and `catalogue/b=b'b'`.
2. For the journal claim line containing `"key": "catalogue/b"`, replace `os.write` with a
   wrapper that writes only the first half of the bytes and returns that short count. Leave
   `os.write` for `a` unchanged.
3. Terminate the child with `os._exit(17)` on `os.chmod(catalogue/b)`, after `os.link` has made
   `b` visible and before ordinary rollback runs.

The child exited 17. The journal contained a complete claim for `a` and a truncated final
claim for `b`. A store opened before the crash returned `catalogue/b` in `snapshot()` with
SHA-256 `3e23e8160039594a33894f6564e1b1348bbd7a0088d42c4acb73eeaed59c009d`.
Reopening the store still returned `b`; recovery deleted the journal and `a`, but ignored the
truncated `b` claim. This leaves a visible, uncommitted object permanently. The injected short
write is a valid `os.write` outcome; treating it as full durable evidence is unsound. Passing
the submitted tests and CI does not cover this path.

The minimum correction is to require a complete, parseable and durable claim before linking
an object: handle or reject short writes before `os.link`, and keep a malformed unresolved
journal fail-closed unless it can be proved that no object was linked without a claim. An
adversarial regression must show that the same short-write/crash sequence cannot expose `b`
through a live or reopened store, and cannot silently discard recovery evidence. Retain the
round-2 visibility, ownership, crash and normal-commit regressions.

F6 remains open. F1, F2, F3 and F5 remain verified closed at the previous head with no code
changes in this range and passing regression tests. F4 and F7 remain open policy questions.
