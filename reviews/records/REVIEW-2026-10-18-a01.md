## Findings, ordered by severity

### F-REVIEW-2026-10-18-a01-1 — Critical: a backfilled revision can masquerade as historically knowable

**Affected:** `docs/standards/observations-and-point-in-time.md:21,37-65,92-97`;
`docs/decisions/HIO-ADR-0004-temporal-semantics.md:26-46`; S1-A5 in
`docs/implementation-plan.md:114`.

**Requirement and evidence:** `as_known_by_market(T)` must return a value that was actually
publicly knowable at T, with evidence for that **value's vintage**. The current contract says a
backfill may get `knowable_at` from a documented schedule and chooses the latest vintage whose
`knowable_at ≤ T`. Suppose an original 2020 release was 100, revised to 110 in 2025, and HIO
first downloads a current-history file in 2026. If the single captured value 110 is stamped
with the 2020 scheduled publication time, a 2021 query returns 110. OBS-INV-003 passes because
the assigned timestamp is ≤ 2021, even though the value was not knowable then. The standard
does not require publisher-vintage or archived-receipt evidence for the value in that query.

**Consequence:** reconstructed history can contaminate predictive tests while claiming
point-in-time integrity. **Corrective direction:** make public availability vintage-specific;
distinguish original release and each revision's actual publication event. A current-only
backfill without historical vintage evidence must not serve an earlier `as_known_by_market(T)`
value, even if the series' release schedule is known. Add a synthetic original/revision/backfill
counterexample to the S1 query tests. Historical knowability that cannot be demonstrated stays
`unknown` or explicitly reconstructed, never silently validated.

### F-REVIEW-2026-10-18-a01-2 — High: the USD cash rule conflicts with its own rate-fixing convention

**Affected:** `docs/standards/return-accounting-and-factor-construction.md:115-127`;
`specs/factors/cash.usd.overnight.yaml:9-18,27-40`;
`specs/factors/fx.usd_cash_vs_aud.excess.yaml:35-59`; S1-A7/A8.

**Requirement and evidence:** RA §3.2 says `rate_{t−1}` is **fixed for the period starting at
`t−1`**, using the last fixing at or before that start. The USD spec instead selects the
latest SOFR rate that was knowable **at the period end**. Consider a Sydney Friday-to-Monday
cash period: the rule can apply Friday SOFR to weekend days because it became knowable by
Monday, although it was not fixed or known when the position began Friday. The proposed
`total_return` and “investable” USD-cash/FX series therefore lack an implementable funding
contract matching the algorithm. The proxy label does not specify who pays the retrospectively
selected rate or how publication lag is handled.

**Consequence:** the S1 core factor can include foresight in an apparently investable carry
leg. **Corrective direction:** choose and specify either an ex-ante achievable cash instrument
with a rate fixed by the investment cutoff, or an ex-post SOFR-linked benchmark with explicit
contractual reset and payment rules and an appropriate proxy/investability label. State the
fixing instant for each accrual day, the NY/Sydney calendar alignment and the S1 factor's
period cutoff. Test a Friday-to-Monday publication-lag case.

### F-REVIEW-2026-10-18-a01-3 — High: release identity and status cannot yet satisfy reproducibility together

**Affected:** `docs/standards/datasets-releases-and-artefacts.md:15-43,52-83`;
`docs/decisions/HIO-ADR-0006-releases-artefacts-publication.md:14-26`; S1-A11.

**Requirement and evidence:** a clean rebuild must reproduce a release's `content_hash`, while
receipts, releases and artefacts are immutable. The manifest contains `release_id` (itself
called a hash), `created_at` and `status`; the envelope likewise contains mutable lifecycle
status. The standard defines canonical JSON bytes, but does not define which fields are hashed
or how an ID avoids hashing itself. If `created_at` or changed status participates in the hash,
an identical rebuild gets a different hash or promotion mutates a content-addressed object.
The catalogue mentions status-change records, but does not say whether the envelope's status
is a birth snapshot or the current effective state.

**Consequence:** S1-A11 and consumer pinning may be impossible or misleading. **Corrective
direction:** define the precise canonical payload/manifest hash domain; exclude run-time
metadata from the deterministic content hash or make it fixed input; separate immutable
birth-state envelope from append-only effective status events, including precedence and
withdrawal semantics. Test rebuild after a later status transition and verify a pinned
consumer sees withdrawal without rewriting the pinned bytes.

### F-REVIEW-2026-10-18-a01-4 — High: the proposed CMT formula drops a coupon on its payment date

**Affected:** `docs/standards/return-accounting-and-factor-construction.md:239-260,428`.

**Requirement and evidence:** §6 correctly states bond TR is dirty-price revaluation **plus a
paid coupon**, but its CMT step 3 defines `R_t = Pdirty_t/Pdirty_{t−1}−1`. On the coupon-day
example in the method table, it reports −1.9608% where the correct TR is zero. Re-striking at
par does not recover the missing cash.

**Consequence:** this would break the planned duration factors in S3 and any approving
return-accounting standard, though it is outside S1. **Corrective direction:** define CMT
wealth as dirty price plus all cash coupons paid during `(t−1,t]`, with an explicit reinvestment
or cash-holding convention, before re-strike. Add coupon-date and non-coupon-date identity
fixtures. The standard should be corrected before HIO-ADR-0007 is approved.

### F-REVIEW-2026-10-18-a01-5 — High: rights gates do not cover each operation the S1 jobs perform

**Affected:** `docs/standards/rights-and-licensing.md:13-53`;
`docs/implementation-plan.md:106-124`; `docs/decisions/HIO-ADR-0005-rights-model.md:22-45`.

**Requirement and evidence:** the rights model records raw retention, internal compute,
derivation, derived storage, external processing and distribution separately, with unknown
meaning deny. The specified S1-A12 gate tests a restricted **publication** and an internal
artefact. It does not require tests that reject capture/retention, derivation, storage or CI
processing when the corresponding permission is denied or unknown. The rights standard's gate
table likewise names publication, quotation, agent use, CI/cloud and termination, but gives no
capture/build operation mapping for `raw_retention`, `internal_compute`, `derivation` and
`derived_storage`.

**Consequence:** a dataset may be ingested or transformed contrary to its rights and only be
stopped at publication. **Corrective direction:** make an operation-to-permission matrix for
fetch/store/build/rebuild/publish/agent/terminate, including conditional grants and evidence;
require fail-closed S1 fixtures for each operation the S1 jobs actually perform. T-0002 must
verify the rights needed before live capture, not only before publication.

### F-REVIEW-2026-10-18-a01-6 — High: S1 cash triangulation calls bill rates “returns” without a bill-return construction

**Affected:** `specs/factors/cash.usd.overnight.yaml:50-60`;
`specs/factors/cash.aud.overnight.yaml:47-57`;
`docs/implementation-plan.md:78-85,119`.

**Requirement and evidence:** independent products may be triangulated only on comparable
defined quantities. S1 inputs list **bill rates**, while the specs call for 4-week/13-week
T-bill “returns” and a 1-month bank-accepted-bill rate “held to maturity.” No bill price,
discount/bond-equivalent yield convention, holding horizon, roll, funding or bill-return spec
is defined. A yield level cannot be silently compared with a realised cash TR. T-0002 checks
source rights and timing, not bill-return construction.

**Consequence:** the mandatory S1-A10 triangulations could validate the wrong quantity or
remain unimplementable. **Corrective direction:** either define bill TR reference constructions
and their cost/term basis, or compare contemporaneous annualised rate levels explicitly as
state/rate triangulation without claiming return agreement. Pre-register the expected
differences and align horizons before S1-A10 is accepted.

### F-REVIEW-2026-10-18-a01-7 — Medium: USD cash compounding bound is false

**Affected:** `specs/factors/cash.usd.overnight.yaml:35-49`; S1-A7.

**Evidence:** the draft requires the within-period difference between simple and
daily-compounded accrual to be `< 1e-8`. Three days at the standard's own illustrative 4.3%
rate give `3×0.043/360 = 0.0003583333333` versus `(1+0.043/360)^3−1 = 0.0003583761360`,
a gap of `4.28026×10⁻⁸`. This is over four times the bound.

**Consequence and correction:** a correct implementation fails its own acceptance test.
Declare a bound as a function of days and rate, or choose exact daily compounding and use it
consistently. Test three- and four-day holiday periods and a plausible rate range.

### F-REVIEW-2026-10-18-a01-8 — Medium: forward and funded-cash excess returns use different denominators

**Affected:** `docs/standards/return-accounting-and-factor-construction.md:159-173`.

**Evidence:** under the stated CIP forward `F=S₀(1+r_h)/(1+r_f)`, funded-cash arithmetic excess
is exactly `(1+r_h)(S_T/F−1)`, not `S_T/F−1`. For `S₀=S_T=1`, `r_h=10%`, `r_f=0`, cash excess
is −10%, while the forward return on its stated `F` notional is −9.0909%. The section attributes
differences to basis and fixing/timing, omitting the certain denominator effect.

**Consequence and correction:** later FX triangulation can mistake a normalisation difference
for market basis. Specify identical initial capital/notional and settlement cashflows before
stating the exact CIP identity; put market basis and timing in separate triangulation terms.
